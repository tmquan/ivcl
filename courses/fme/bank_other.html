Problem 1

BJT

Problem

A voltage amplifier with a gain of 10 listens to a microphone and drives a speaker. The microphone is a source of \(10\,\mathrm{mV}\) peak-to-peak with a series resistance of \(200\,\Omega\); the speaker is an \(8\,\Omega\) resistor. The amplifier has an input resistance \(R_{in}\) to ground and an output resistance \(R_{amp}\) in series with its ideal output voltage. (a) How much of the microphone signal appears across the amplifier input if \(R_{in}=2\,\mathrm{k}\Omega\) or \(500\,\Omega\)? (b) What fraction of the amplifier output voltage reaches the speaker if \(R_{amp}=10\,\Omega\) or \(2\,\Omega\)?

Given

\(v_m=10\,\mathrm{mV_{pp}}\), \(R_S=200\,\Omega\), \(R_L=8\,\Omega\), \(A_v=10\), \(R_{in}=2\,\mathrm{k}\Omega\) or \(500\,\Omega\), \(R_{amp}=10\,\Omega\) or \(2\,\Omega\)

Answer 1

Input interface. The microphone resistance \(R_S\) and the amplifier input resistance \(R_{in}\) form a voltage divider: \(v_{in}\) \(=\dfrac{R_{in}}{R_{in}+R_S}\,v_m\).

Answer 2

(a) \(R_{in}=2\,\mathrm{k}\Omega\). \(v_{in}\) \(=\dfrac{2000}{2000+200}v_m\) \(=0.909\,v_m\), i.e. \(9.09\,\mathrm{mV_{pp}}\) of the \(10\,\mathrm{mV_{pp}}\): a loss of only about 9 %.

Answer 3

(a) \(R_{in}=500\,\Omega\). \(v_{in}\) \(=\dfrac{500}{500+200}v_m\) \(=0.714\,v_m\), i.e. \(7.14\,\mathrm{mV_{pp}}\): nearly 30 % is lost. A large input resistance is desirable.

Answer 4

Output interface. The ideal amplifier output \(v_{amp}\) sits behind \(R_{amp}\) and drives the speaker \(R_L\): \(v_{out}\) \(=\dfrac{R_L}{R_L+R_{amp}}\,v_{amp}\).

Answer 5

(b) \(R_{amp}=10\,\Omega\). \(v_{out}\) \(=\dfrac{8}{8+10}v_{amp}\) \(=0.444\,v_{amp}\): more than half of the signal is lost.

Answer 6

(b) \(R_{amp}=2\,\Omega\). \(v_{out}\) \(=\dfrac{8}{8+2}v_{amp}\) \(=0.800\,v_{amp}\). A small output resistance is desirable.

Answer 7

Numerical illustration. With an ideal input (\(v_{amp}\) \(=10\times10\,\mathrm{mV_{pp}}\) \(=100\,\mathrm{mV_{pp}}\)) the speaker would see \(44.4\,\mathrm{mV_{pp}}\) or \(80.0\,\mathrm{mV_{pp}}\) respectively.

Result

(a) \(0.909\,v_m\) for \(2\,\mathrm{k}\Omega\), \(0.714\,v_m\) for \(500\,\Omega\); (b) \(0.444\,v_{amp}\) for \(10\,\Omega\), \(0.800\,v_{amp}\) for \(2\,\Omega\)

Problem 2

BJT

Problem

A microphone signal is amplified by a single common-emitter stage (collector resistor \(R_C\), collector current \(I_C\)) whose supply \(V_{CC}\) comes from a rectifier. The rectifier output is not perfectly constant but carries a ripple \(v_r\) at twice the line frequency. The loudspeaker plays a loud steady low-frequency hum. Explain where it comes from and how strongly the ripple reaches the output.

Given

\(V_{CC}\) with ripple \(v_r\) (frequency \(2f_\mathrm{line}\)); \(V_{out}=V_{CC}-R_CI_C\)

Answer 1

Output voltage of the stage. The collector node sits at the supply minus the drop across the load: \(V_{out}=V_{CC}-R_CI_C\).

Answer 2

Bias current is set by the input. \(I_C\) is fixed by the base bias, not by \(V_{CC}\) (ignoring the Early effect), so a change of \(V_{CC}\) leaves \(R_CI_C\) unchanged.

Answer 3

Effect of the ripple. If \(V_{CC}\) moves by \(v_r\), then \(V_{out}\) moves by \(v_r\) as well: \(\Delta V_{out}/\Delta V_{CC}=1\). The output therefore follows the supply with unity gain.

Answer 4

Two components at the output. \(v_{out}=A_vv_{in}+v_r\): the amplified microphone signal plus the whole ripple.

Answer 5

Source of the hum. The ripple is a steady component at \(2f_\mathrm{line}\) (100 or 120 Hz), which the loudspeaker reproduces as hum. The cause is that \(V_{out}\) is measured against ground while \(V_{CC}\) is not constant; the output is not insensitive to the supply.

Answer 6

. Measure the output between this node and a second node that carries the same ripple (a duplicate stage), so the ripple cancels in the difference: this is the idea of the differential amplifier.

Result

Hum comes from supply ripple reaching \(V_{out}\) with gain \(1\): \(v_{out}=A_vv_{in}+v_r\)

Problem 3

BJT

Problem

A transformer with a center-tapped secondary delivers two signals \(+v_{in}\) and \(-v_{in}\) that are equal and opposite about the center tap. With the tap grounded their common-mode (average) level is 0 V. How must the transformer be connected so the two outputs have a common-mode level of \(+2\,\mathrm{V}\)?

Given

Required common-mode level \(V_{CM}=2\,\mathrm{V}\)

Answer 1

Common-mode level. The CM level of two outputs \(V_1,V_2\) that swing equally and oppositely is their dc average, which is the dc level of the center tap.

Answer 2

Outputs with the tap grounded. \(V_1=+v_{in}\), \(V_2=-v_{in}\), so \((V_1+V_2)/2=0\).

Answer 3

Raise the tap. The windings only fix the voltage of each end relative to the tap. Tying the center tap to \(+2\,\mathrm{V}\) shifts both ends by \(2\,\mathrm{V}\): \(V_1=2\,\mathrm{V}+v_{in}\), \(V_2=2\,\mathrm{V}-v_{in}\).

Answer 4

Check. Average \(=2\,\mathrm{V}\) and difference \(V_1-V_2=2v_{in}\) unchanged.

Result

Connect the center tap to a \(+2\,\mathrm{V}\) source: \(V_{CM}=2\,\mathrm{V}\)

Problem 4

BJT

Problem

An antenna is modelled by its Thevenin equivalent: a sinusoidal source \(V_0\cos\omega t\) in series with an output resistance \(R_{out}\). A load resistor \(R_L\) is connected across it. Find the average power delivered to \(R_L\) and sketch it as a function of \(R_L\).

Given

\(v_s=V_0\cos\omega t\), source resistance \(R_{out}\), load \(R_L\)

Answer 1

Voltage divider. The load voltage is \(v_L\) \(=\dfrac{R_L}{R_{out}+R_L}V_0\cos\omega t\).

Answer 2

Instantaneous power. \(p(t)\) \(=\dfrac{v_L^2}{R_L}\) \(=\dfrac{R_LV_0^2}{(R_{out}+R_L)^2}\cos^2\omega t\).

Answer 3

Average over a period. The mean of \(\cos^2\omega t\) is \(1/2\), so \(P_{av}\) \(=\dfrac{V_0^2R_L}{2(R_{out}+R_L)^2}\).

Answer 4

Maximum. \(\dfrac{dP_{av}}{dR_L}\propto (R_{out}+R_L)-2R_L\) \(=0\) gives \(R_L=R_{out}\) and \(P_{max}=\dfrac{V_0^2}{8R_{out}}\).

Answer 5

Shape of the plot. \(P_{av}=0\) at \(R_L=0\), rises linearly (\(\approx V_0^2R_L/2R_{out}^2\)) for \(R_L\ll R_{out}\), peaks at \(R_L=R_{out}\), then falls as \(\approx V_0^2/2R_L\) for \(R_L\gg R_{out}\). It is symmetric on a logarithmic \(R_L\) axis: \(P_{av}(R_L)=P_{av}(R_{out}^2/R_L)\).

Result

\(P_{av}\) \(=\dfrac{V_0^2R_L}{2(R_{out}+R_L)^2}\), maximum \(V_0^2/(8R_{out})\) at \(R_L=R_{out}\)

Problem 5

BJT

Problem

Find the small-signal input resistance of three diode networks, all diodes forward-biased (each diode is a small-signal resistance \(r_D=V_T/I_D\)). (a) Input through diode \(D_1\), then \(R_1\) to ground. (b) Input through \(D_1\) to a node X; from X, \(R_1\) to ground and diode \(D_2\) (anode at X) to ground. (c) Input through a diode to node X; between X and node Y, \(R_1\) in parallel with diode \(D_2\) (anode at X); from Y a second diode \(D_1\) (anode at Y) to ground.

Given

Diodes forward-biased; \(r_{D}=V_T/I_D\)

Answer 1

Diode model. Each forward-biased diode is replaced by \(r_{Dj}=V_T/I_{Dj}\); a constant voltage source is not needed for small signals.

Answer 2

(a) Series path. \(R_{in}=r_{D1}+R_1\).

Answer 3

(b) Series then parallel. \(R_{in}\) \(=r_{D1}+(R_1\parallel r_{D2})\).

Answer 4

(c) Same current in the two series diodes. The input diode and the diode to ground carry the same current, so both have \(r_{D1}=V_T/I_{D1}\).

Answer 5

(c) Total. \(R_{in}\) \(=2r_{D1}+(R_1\parallel r_{D2})\).

Answer 6

Numerical check. With \(I_{D1}=1\,\mathrm{mA}\), \(I_{D2}=0.4\,\mathrm{mA}\), \(R_1=100\,\Omega\): \(r_{D1}=26\,\Omega\), \(r_{D2}=65\,\Omega\), (c) gives \(91.4\,\Omega\) by formula and by nodal analysis.

Result

(a) \(r_{D1}+R_1\); (b) \(r_{D1}+R_1\parallel r_{D2}\); (c) \(2r_{D1}+R_1\parallel r_{D2}\)

Problem 6

BJT

Problem

An op amp has its non-inverting input grounded; a resistor \(R_1\) runs from \(V_{REF}\) to the inverting input; npn \(Q_1\) has its base grounded, its collector at the inverting input and its emitter at the op-amp output \(V_1\) (a logarithmic amplifier). \(V_1\) drives the base of npn \(Q_2\), whose emitter sits at the negative terminal of a battery \(V_B\) (positive terminal grounded) and whose collector draws \(I_X\). Explain what the circuit does.

Given

\(I_{REF}=V_{REF}/R_1\), \(Q_1\), \(Q_2\) with saturation currents \(I_{S1}\), \(I_{S2}\), battery \(V_B\)

Answer 1

Log amplifier. The inverting input is a virtual ground, so \(I_{REF}=V_{REF}/R_1\) flows into the collector of \(Q_1\) (op-amp input current zero). With base at 0 and emitter at \(V_1\): \(V_{BE1}\) \(=-V_1\) \(=V_T\ln\dfrac{I_{REF}}{I_{S1}}\), so \(V_1=-V_T\ln\dfrac{I_{REF}}{I_{S1}}\).

Answer 2

Base-emitter voltage of \(Q_2\). \(V_{BE2}\) \(=V_1-(-V_B)\) \(=V_B-V_T\ln\dfrac{I_{REF}}{I_{S1}}\)

Answer 3

Output current. \(I_X\) \(=I_{S2}\exp\dfrac{V_{BE2}}{V_T}\) \(=\dfrac{I_{S1}I_{S2}}{I_{REF}}\exp\dfrac{V_B}{V_T}\)

Answer 4

What happens. This is not a current copy. \(I_X\) is inversely proportional to \(I_{REF}\) and depends exponentially on the battery voltage and on \(I_{S1}I_{S2}\) (hence strongly on temperature). Without the battery \(V_{BE2}=V_1<0\) and \(Q_2\) is off.

Answer 5

Numerical check. With \(I_S=6\times10^{-17}\,\mathrm{A}\), \(I_{REF}=0.1\,\mathrm{mA}\), \(V_B=1.4\,\mathrm{V}\): \(V_1=-0.732\,\mathrm{V}\), \(V_{BE2}=0.668\,\mathrm{V}\), \(I_X=8.74\,\mu\mathrm{A}\); doubling \(I_{REF}\) halves \(I_X\).

Result

\(I_X\) \(=\dfrac{I_{S1}I_{S2}}{I_{REF}}e^{V_B/V_T}\): inversely proportional to \(I_{REF}\), set by the battery and temperature; not a mirror

Problem 7

Problem

With \(I_1=I_0\cos\omega t+I_0\) and \(I_2=-I_0\cos\omega t+I_0\), sketch \(V_X\) and \(V_Y\) and give their peak-to-peak swings and common-mode level.

Given

\(I_1=I_0\cos\omega t+I_0\), \(I_2=-I_0\cos\omega t+I_0\), equal \(R_C\)

Answer 1

Node X. All of \(I_1\) flows down through \(R_C\): \(V_X=R_CI_1=I_0R_C(1+\cos\omega t)\).

Answer 2

Node Y. \(V_Y=R_CI_2=I_0R_C(1-\cos\omega t)\).

Answer 3

Waveforms. \(V_X\) is a cosine between \(0\) and \(2I_0R_C\) (maximum at \(t=0\)); \(V_Y\) is the same cosine inverted, between \(2I_0R_C\) and \(0\).

Answer 4

Swings. Each node swings \(2I_0R_C\) peak to peak; \(V_X-V_Y=2I_0R_C\cos\omega t\) swings \(4I_0R_C\).

Answer 5

CM level. \((V_X+V_Y)/2=I_0R_C\), constant.

Result

\(V_{X,Y}=I_0R_C(1\pm\cos\omega t)\); each \(2I_0R_C\) pp, CM level \(I_0R_C\), differential swing \(4I_0R_C\)

With \(I_1=I_0\cos\omega t+I_0\) and \(I_2=-I_0\cos\omega t+I_0\), sketch \(V_X\), \(V_Y\) and \(V_P\) versus time and give the swings and the CM level.

\(I_1=I_0\cos\omega t+I_0\), \(I_2=-I_0\cos\omega t+I_0\), \(R_1\), equal \(R_C\)

Node P. \(V_P=V_{CC}-2I_0R_1\): a constant (flat line).

Node X. \(V_X\) \(=V_P-R_CI_1\) \(=V_{CC}-2I_0R_1-I_0R_C(1+\cos\omega t)\).

Node Y. \(V_Y\) \(=V_{CC}-2I_0R_1-I_0R_C(1-\cos\omega t)\).

Swings and CM. Each swings \(2I_0R_C\) pp, in opposite phase; CM level \(V_{CC}-2I_0R_1-I_0R_C\); \(V_X-V_Y=-2I_0R_C\cos\omega t\).

\(V_P=V_{CC}-2I_0R_1\) (constant); \(V_{X,Y}\) \(=V_{CC}-2I_0R_1-I_0R_C(1\pm\cos\omega t)\); \(2I_0R_C\) pp each; CM \(V_{CC}-2I_0R_1-I_0R_C\)

Problem 11

BJT

Problem

Sketch \(V_X\), \(V_Y\), give swings and levels, and decide whether \(V_X\) and \(V_Y\) are truly differential signals.

Given

\(I_1=I_0\cos\omega t+I_0\), \(I_2=-I_0\cos\omega t+0.8I_0\), equal \(R_C\)

Answer 1

Node X. \(V_X=I_0R_C(1+\cos\omega t)\): swings \(0\) to \(2I_0R_C\), average \(I_0R_C\).

Answer 2

Node Y. \(V_Y=I_0R_C(0.8-\cos\omega t)\): swings \(-0.2I_0R_C\) to \(1.8I_0R_C\), average \(0.8I_0R_C\).

Answer 3

Swings. Both swing \(2I_0R_C\) pp in opposite phase; \(V_X-V_Y\) \(=2I_0R_C\cos\omega t+0.2I_0R_C\).

Answer 4

Definition. Differential signals must vary by equal and opposite amounts around the same average (CM) level.

Answer 5

Verdict. The varying parts are equal and opposite, but the average levels differ by \(0.2I_0R_C\), so \(V_X-V_Y\) carries a dc offset of \(0.2I_0R_C\): strictly, \(X\) and \(Y\) are not true differential signals.

Result

\(V_X=I_0R_C(1+\cos\omega t)\), \(V_Y=I_0R_C(0.8-\cos\omega t)\); both \(2I_0R_C\) pp; averages \(I_0R_C\) and \(0.8I_0R_C\), so not truly differential

Problem 12

BJT

Problem

(a) an extra constant source \(I_T\) from \(Y\) to ground; (b) a constant source \(I_T\) from \(X\) to \(Y\); (c) a resistor \(R_P\) between \(X\) and \(Y\); (d) a resistor \(R_P\) from \(Y\) to ground. Plot \(V_X\) and \(V_Y\) versus time.

Given

\(I_1=I_0\cos\omega t+I_0\), \(I_2=-I_0\cos\omega t+I_0\), equal \(R_C\), constant \(I_T\), resistor \(R_P\)

Answer 1

–

Answer 2

(a) Node Y. \(R_C\) now carries \(I_2+I_T\): \(V_Y\) \(=V_{CC}-I_TR_C-I_0R_C(1-\cos\omega t)\); same swing \(2I_0R_C\) pp, shifted down by \(I_TR_C\).

Answer 3

(b) KCL at X and Y. \(I_{RC,X}=I_1+I_T\), \(I_{RC,Y}=I_2-I_T\).

Answer 4

(b) Waveforms. \(V_X\) \(=V_{CC}-I_TR_C-I_0R_C(1+\cos\omega t)\), \(V_Y\) \(=V_{CC}+I_TR_C-I_0R_C(1-\cos\omega t)\): swings unchanged, averages split by \(2I_TR_C\), CM level still \(V_{CC}-I_0R_C\).

Answer 5

(c) Sum of KCLs. \(2V_{CC}-V_X-V_Y\) \(=R_C(I_1+I_2)\) \(=2I_0R_C\): CM level \(V_{CC}-I_0R_C\) unchanged (\(R_P\) carries no CM current).

Answer 6

(c) Difference. \((V_X-V_Y)\left(\dfrac{1}{R_C}+\dfrac{2}{R_P}\right)\) \(=-(I_1-I_2)\), so \(V_X-V_Y\) \(=-2I_0\cos\omega t\,\left(R_C\parallel\dfrac{R_P}{2}\right)\).

Answer 7

(c) Waveforms. \(V_{X,Y}\) \(=V_{CC}-I_0R_C\mp I_0\left(R_C\parallel\dfrac{R_P}{2}\right)\cos\omega t\): smaller swing, same CM.

Answer 8

(d) Node Y. KCL: \(\dfrac{V_{CC}-V_Y}{R_C}\) \(=I_2+\dfrac{V_Y}{R_P}\), so \(V_Y\) \(=\dfrac{R_P}{R_C+R_P}\left[V_{CC}-I_0R_C(1-\cos\omega t)\right]\).

Answer 9

(d) Waveforms. \(V_X\) as in (a); \(V_Y\) has amplitude scaled by \(R_P/(R_C+R_P)\) and a lower average \(\dfrac{R_P}{R_C+R_P}(V_{CC}-I_0R_C)\), so \(X\) and \(Y\) are no longer symmetric.

Answer 10

Numeric check. With \(V_{CC}=2.5\,\mathrm{V}\), \(I_0=0.5\,\mathrm{mA}\), \(R_C=1\,\mathrm{k}\Omega\), \(I_T=0.2\,\mathrm{mA}\), \(R_P=2\,\mathrm{k}\Omega\), solving the node equations at several instants reproduces every formula above.

Result

(a) \(V_Y\) shifted down by \(I_TR_C\); (b) \(V_X\) down, \(V_Y\) up by \(I_TR_C\); (c) swing reduced to \(I_0(R_C\parallel R_P/2)\) amplitude, CM \(V_{CC}-I_0R_C\); (d) \(V_Y\) \(=\frac{R_P}{R_C+R_P}[V_{CC}-I_0R_C(1-\cos\omega t)]\)

Problem 13

BJT

Problem

In (a) nothing else is connected to \(P\); in (b) a resistor \(R_1\) goes from \(P\) to ground; in (c) a constant current source (labelled \(I_1\), called \(I_T\) in the text) sinks current from \(P\) to ground. With \(V_1=V_0\cos\omega t+V_0\) and \(V_2=-V_0\cos\omega t+V_0\), plot \(V_P\) versus time.

Given

\(V_1=V_0\cos\omega t+V_0\), \(V_2=-V_0\cos\omega t+V_0\), equal \(R_C\), \(R_1\), constant \(I_T\)

Answer 1

(a) Divider. No current leaves \(P\), so the same current flows from \(V_1\) to \(V_2\) and \(V_P=\dfrac{V_1+V_2}{2}=V_0\): constant.

Answer 2

(b) KCL at P. \(\dfrac{V_1-V_P}{R_C}+\dfrac{V_2-V_P}{R_C}\) \(=\dfrac{V_P}{R_1}\)

Answer 3

(b) Result. \(V_P\) \(=\dfrac{(V_1+V_2)R_1}{2R_1+R_C}\) \(=\dfrac{2R_1}{2R_1+R_C}V_0\): constant.

Answer 4

(c) KCL at P. \(\dfrac{V_1-V_P}{R_C}+\dfrac{V_2-V_P}{R_C}\) \(=I_T\), so \(V_P=V_0-\dfrac{I_TR_C}{2}\): constant.

Answer 5

Interpretation. In every case \(V_P\) depends only on \(V_1+V_2=2V_0\), which does not vary: the differential components cancel at \(P\), the basis of the virtual ground of a differential pair.

Result

Problem 20

MOSFET

Problem

A MOSFET is in pinch-off near the drain. The relation \(I=Q_d\,v\) (current = mobile charge per unit length times carrier velocity) implies that if the current is constant, charge density and velocity change in opposite directions. How should this be interpreted at the pinch-off point, where the charge density tends to zero?

Given

\(I_D=Q_d(x)\,v(x)\) constant along the channel

Answer 1

What the equation says. Since \(I_D=Q_d v\) is the same at every \(x\), a falling \(Q_d\) must be compensated by a rising \(v\); as \(Q_d\to 0\) at pinch-off, \(v=I_D/Q_d\) would have to tend to infinity.

Answer 2

Why that cannot be literal. Carrier velocity is limited (it saturates near \(10^7\,\mathrm{cm/s}\) in silicon), so the charge density never truly reaches zero: a small but finite density of electrons moving very fast carries the same current.

Answer 3

Where the model breaks. The relation \(Q_d=WC_{ox}(V_{GS}-V-V_{TH})\) assumes the vertical field dominates (gradual-channel approximation). Near pinch-off the lateral field becomes large, the approximation fails, and the “zero charge” is an artifact of the simple model.

Answer 4

Beyond pinch-off. Past the pinch-off point the electrons leave the inversion layer and are swept through the depletion region to the drain by the strong lateral field, much as carriers cross the collector junction of a bipolar transistor; the current is set by how many electrons reach the pinch-off point, which is why \(I_D\) barely depends on \(V_{DS}\).

Result

The charge density becomes very small but not zero, and the carriers move very fast (up to the saturation velocity), so \(Q_d v\) stays equal to \(I_D\); beyond pinch-off the high lateral field sweeps the carriers to the drain.

Answer 3

At \(V_{SB}=4\,\mathrm{V}\). \(V_t\) \(=1+0.5\left(\sqrt{4.6}-\sqrt{0.6}\right)\) \(=1+0.5(2.145-0.775)\) \(=1.69\,\mathrm{V}\).

Answer 4

Thicker oxide. \(\gamma\) \(=\sqrt{2q\epsilon_sN_A}/C_{ox}\), and \(C_{ox}\propto1/t_{ox}\), so \(\gamma\) becomes \(4\times0.5=2\,\mathrm{V^{1/2}}\).

Answer 5

New threshold. \(V_t\) \(=1+2\left(\sqrt{4.6}-\sqrt{0.6}\right)\) \(=1+2(1.370)\) \(=3.74\,\mathrm{V}\).

Result

\(V_t\) rises from \(1\,\mathrm{V}\) to \(1.69\,\mathrm{V}\); with \(4\times t_{ox}\) it reaches \(3.74\,\mathrm{V}\)

Problem 30

MOSFET

Problem

Moore-type scaling: a chip made in a \(5\,\mu\mathrm{m}\) process holds \(20{,}000\) transistors. By how much does the transistor count grow on a chip of the same area if the process is scaled to \(32\,\mathrm{nm}\) (device area shrinks as the square of the linear scaling factor)?

Given

Process \(5\,\mu\mathrm{m}\to32\,\mathrm{nm}\), \(N_0=20{,}000\) transistors on the same chip area

Answer 1

Scaling factor. \(S\) \(=\dfrac{5\,\mu\mathrm{m}}{32\,\mathrm{nm}}\) \(=\dfrac{5000}{32}\) \(=156.25\).

Answer 2

Device area. Each device occupies \(1/S^2\) of the old area, so \(S^2=24{,}414\) times as many devices fit on the same area.

Answer 3

Transistor count. \(N\) \(=20{,}000\times S^2\) \(=20{,}000\times156.25^2\) \(=4.88\times10^8\), about \(488\) million.

Result

\(N\approx4.88\times10^8\) (488 million) transistors

Problem 31

MOSFET

Problem

A PMOS transistor has \(\lvert V_{t0}\rvert\) \(=0.7\,\mathrm{V}\), \(\gamma=0.5\,\mathrm{V^{1/2}}\), \(2\phi_f=0.75\,\mathrm{V}\). Find its threshold voltage when \(V_{BS}\) is such that \(\lvert V_{SB}\rvert=3\,\mathrm{V}\).

Given

\(\lvert V_{t0}\rvert\) \(=0.7\,\mathrm{V}\), \(\gamma=0.5\,\mathrm{V^{1/2}}\), \(2\phi_f=0.75\,\mathrm{V}\), \(\lvert V_{SB}\rvert=3\,\mathrm{V}\)

Answer 1

Body-effect formula. For a PMOS the magnitudes obey \(\lvert V_t\rvert\) \(=\lvert V_{t0}\rvert+\gamma\left(\sqrt{2\phi_f+\lvert V_{SB}\rvert}-\sqrt{2\phi_f}\right)\).

Answer 2

Substitute. \(\lvert V_t\rvert\) \(=0.7+0.5\left(\sqrt{3.75}-\sqrt{0.75}\right)\) \(=0.7+0.5(1.936-0.866)\).

Answer 3

Result. \(\lvert V_t\rvert\) \(=1.235\) \(\approx1.24\,\mathrm{V}\), so \(V_t=-1.24\,\mathrm{V}\) (the PMOS threshold is negative).

Result

\(V_t=-1.24\,\mathrm{V}\)
