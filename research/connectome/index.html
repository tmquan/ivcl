Connectome Agent#

An agent that reconstructs an electron-microscopy (EM) volume inside Isaac Sim. It holds the tissue as a set of 3D Gaussians, looks at it through a camera that cuts sections, and edits the Gaussians until they explain the image, the segmentation and the class of every structure.

Three words are used in a fixed sense throughout. Voxel is the image grid. Frame is the medial axes of one connected component, with a radius at every point. Graph is the set of components and their links. A 2D image from the camera is called a view, never a frame.

Note

This is a design, written on 23 November 2026. It replaces an earlier design built around a flood-filling network. One part has been run: a scene of the CellMap crop 313 was built and rendered on the lab’s DGX Spark with the Kit runtime of Isaac Sim. Everything else is to be built. Numbers marked estimate are arithmetic, not measurements.

Multi-scale

One object ties the four together: the Gaussian. A Gaussian is an ellipsoid of stained material with a centre, a shape, an intensity, an identity and a class.

  • It can be drawn into voxels. The Gaussians of one connected component, with their neighbour links, are its Frame. The components and their contacts are the Graph. So it joins the three representations.

  • Its intensity is trained by the raw image, its identity by the segmentation, its class by the class labels. So it carries the three kinds of supervision.

  • Its centre and shape are the geometry; the ratio of its axes is the anisotropy. So it is the feature.

  • Small Gaussians merge into large ones and large ones split into small ones. So it has a scale, and a pyramid of them is a mipmap.

The agent is the loop that edits this set.

Decisions#

#

Decision

Turned down

Why, and what it costs

D1

The state of the agent is a set of anisotropic 3D Gaussians.

A voxel canvas per object (flood filling).

One primitive serves Voxel, Frame and Graph, and is trained by all three kinds of supervision. The cost: a Gaussian is smooth, so the exact membrane position comes from a final voxel step.

D2

The agent’s view is a slab: the sum of everything inside one section thickness. No depth order, no occlusion.

Perspective splatting with alpha blending, as in novel-view synthesis.

An EM image is a thin slice of tissue, not a view of a surface. The slab integral of a Gaussian has a closed form. The cost: the standard splat renderers cannot be used for training; the project writes its own in Warp.

D3

Isaac Sim is the runtime. One Isaac Sim process per GPU holds many blocks.

A separate core with Isaac Sim as a viewer.

Required. It also gives cameras, annotators, USD layers and Warp in one process. The cost: the build must work, and each process takes tens of seconds to start.

D4

Continuous values (centre, shape, intensity) are fitted by gradient descent. Discrete edits (split, merge, link, label) are chosen by a policy.

A policy that outputs everything; or optimisation only.

Gradients are exact and cheap for continuous values and do not exist for structure. The cost: two mechanisms to keep in step.

D5

Every length is in nanometres. The section thickness is a parameter of the camera.

Working in voxels.

One model then serves 4 × 4 × 40 nm sections and 8 nm isotropic blocks.

D6

The stage is the record. The data, the agent’s edits and the reviewer’s edits are separate USD layers.

A separate event log.

A reviewer’s layer overrides the agent’s without changing it, and removing a layer undoes it. The cost: layers record the result of edits, not their order; the order is kept in a small side table.

D7

The policy’s view encoder can be swapped. The first one is a small convolutional network; Cosmos 3 Edge is an option for later.

Cosmos in the main loop from the start.

A sequence of views along a Frame is a video, which suits Cosmos, but nothing in the design depends on it.

Build order#

Each milestone has one test.

Milestone

Data

Done when

M0

Isaac Sim build finished; scene viewer

CellMap crop 313

SimulationApp renders a non-empty image on the Spark. (The viewer already renders through the Kit route.)

M1

Voxel store, mipmap, slab camera

CREMI A

A view from the camera at any level equals the same slice computed with numpy.

M2

Slab renderer in Warp, with gradients; fit to raw

A 2 µm crop of CREMI A

Gradients match finite differences. The fitted Gaussians reproduce the image above a set quality, with the count reported.

M3

Frames from the true labels; Graph; back to voxels

CREMI A

The segmentation rebuilt from those Frames has adapted Rand error below 0.02 against the labels. This shows the representation is sufficient.

M4

The multi-scale cycle

CREMI A, SNEMI3D

Same quality as M2 and M3 with fewer Gaussians and fewer steps.

M5

The policy for discrete edits

CREMI, SNEMI3D

Scores on the held-out sections no worse than a baseline affinity network with watershed.

M6

Class types: clefts and synapses; organelles

CREMI; CellMap crops

Cleft and synapse scores on CREMI; per-class scores on held-out CellMap crops.

M7

Vectorised environment, layers, review in the window

CREMI

A reviewer’s edit in the Isaac Sim window overrides the agent, and removing the reviewer’s layer restores the agent’s result.

M8

Blocks and stitching

A 100 µm cube of MICrONS

Killing workers at random does not change the result.

M3 comes before any learning on purpose. If Frames made from perfect labels cannot be turned back into a good segmentation, the design is wrong, and it is cheap to find that out first.
